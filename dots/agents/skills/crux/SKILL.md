# Crux

The crux is the most important part of a challenge that can actually be
solved soon. Name it before solving anything. The problem is the user's
argument, or the task at hand.

1. **List.** Write down every concern, obstacle and opportunity. Split items
   that are really several problems; group the related ones.
2. **Filter.** Keep the items that are both _critical_ (solving it changes the
   outcome) and _addressable_ (real progress is possible soon with what's at
   hand). A critical problem nobody can move yet is a later crux.
3. **Diagnose.** Ask "what is going on here?" and "why is this hard?" until the
   structure of the difficulty shows. Make assumptions explicit, look for
   asymmetries and others' inertia, try analogies and other viewpoints, and
   find the constraints that are self-imposed.
4. **Name the crux.** One sentence: the knot that, once cut, makes the rest
   tractable. Done when you can say why it is hard and why it beats every
   other item on the list.
5. **Act coherently.** A few actions that all attack the crux and reinforce
   each other. Everything else is deferred, by name.

Report the crux, why it is hard, the actions, and what was deferred. After the
crux falls, diagnose again: the next one is different.
